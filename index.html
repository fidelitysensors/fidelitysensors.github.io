Where Expertise Meets Engineering

Architecture-to-Target Matching

The right sensing architecture depends on the target analyte, the sample matrix, the testing frequency, the performance requirements and more. The wrong choices early on compound every downstream problem. I help teams evaluate and select the architecture most likely to succeed before significant resources are committed.

Design for Manufacturability

A biosensor that works in the lab but can't be made consistently at scale isn't a product. I work with development teams to identify manufacturability constraints early and align design choices with process capabilities to reduce the gap between prototype performance and product performance.

Limit-of-Detection Optimization

Reaching the required LOD is often the central challenge in biosensor development. I bring a systematic, mechanistic approach to signal amplification, background suppression, and assay optimization. My approach is grounded in the physics of the system and the measurement, not just empirical iteration.

Noise & Interference Mitigation

Real-world samples are complex. Non-specific binding, matrix effects, electrochemical interferences, and environmental variables can all degrade performance in ways that aren't visible in clean buffer or model systems. This is often one of the hardest stages of development to address because the root causes are frequently unknown, and the challenges typically arrive late in development. I help teams anticipate, characterize, and engineer around these challenges.

Third-Party Verification

Independent verification for any sensing platform

Assays that work perfectly on the bench often fail during scale-up or tech transfer. This failure risks eroding trust, delaying timelines, and or both. Third-party verification eliminates this risk by providing objective proof of your system's robustness. Whether you are advancing internal development or preparing for an external tech transfer, I engineer custom validation protocols driven by regulatory requirements and tailored to your exact analyte and matrix. This ensures your performance claims hold up under scrutiny, preserving stakeholder confidence and keeping your commercialization timeline on track.

What every verification covers

Intended use and acceptance criteria

We define what the sensor must do, in which matrix, and what counts as success. All of it goes into a written protocol before any testing begins.

Detection capability

Limit of detection, limit of quantification, or decision cutoff, depending on whether your sensor reports a number, a category, or a yes/no result.

Selectivity and interference

Testing against structurally related compounds, matrix components, and the sample variables that matter for your application, including multiple individual sample sources.

Precision and reproducibility

Repeatability and between-run reproducibility across the claimed range, including sensor-to-sensor and lot-to-lot variation.

Stability and robustness

Shelf life, in-use stability, and sensitivity to temperature, timing, and handling, using conditions that match real-world use.

Reference method comparison

Agreement with an established method such as chromatography, a validated immunoassay, or standard addition using a tracable standard, so your results are anchored to something others already trust.

Tailored to your sensing modality

The framework stays the same, but what gets tested and how results are expressed changes with the technology. Three examples:

Electrochemical aptamer-based sensing

  • Calibration and signal gain in the intended matrix and at the intended temperature
  • Dynamic range relative to aptamer affinity, plus response time and reversibility
  • Baseline drift and fouling over the intended measurement duration, including how well drift correction works
  • Sensor-to-sensor reproducibility across fabrication batches

Qualitative and semi-quantitative colorimetric sensing

  • Behavior of samples near the cutoff, to map the uncertain zone between clear negatives and clear positives
  • False-positive and false-negative rates against a reference method
  • For semi-quantitative formats, agreement of color-scale categories with reference concentrations
  • Reader variability across observers, lighting, and smartphone cameras, plus color stability over the read window

Fluorescence sensing

  • Detection and quantification limits based on blank variability in the real matrix
  • Matrix autofluorescence, quenching, and inner-filter effects
  • Photobleaching and signal stability during readout
  • Instrument-to-instrument and reader-setting sensitivity, plus linear range and saturation

What you receive

  • A written validation protocol, agreed with you before any testing begins
  • A verification report that presents all results, including any that fall outside the predefined criteria
  • Records of reference standards, reagent and sensor lots, and sample handling detailed enough to reconstruct the study

Study designs draw on established validation principles, such as FDA's ICH M10 and biomarker bioanalytical method validation guidances and CLSI evaluation protocols, selected to fit your target, modality, and intended use. These guidances are nonbinding and were not written for every sensor type, so I adapt them rather than apply them literally. Verification results describe how your sensor performed; they are not an FDA clearance, approval, or endorsement.
